# Lessons learned

Rules learned on real videos, on top of the method. Only current rules live here. When the user changes one, replace the line and delete the old version (git keeps the history). Add new ones with the date.

Where a rule is a taste choice it says "default": the user may change it.

## Money and order
1. **No paid generation without an explicit "yes" on the exact thing.** Before the yes, name the model, the number of jobs and the credits from Higgsfield's estimate. Quote credits, not dollars. A connected account or "I want to learn" is not permission to spend.
2. **Order: story → preproduction (script, 3x3 storyboard, "ok") → reference images → previz → video.** Don't talk about video and credits until the story is approved.
3. **A weak idea must die before money is spent,** in preproduction or at the previz.
4. **Generate only by the method.** Every price message carries the stage checklist with yes/no per item, and any move outside the method (another model, no previz, no prompt template) is called a deviation before the yes. A real case: all shots of a reel were made as separate 4-second clips from start frames only, without previz and with free-text prompts, to save credits. Cars didn't brake, people didn't turn, hits didn't land, and the cut looked like a slideshow. The credits were wasted.
5. **Upscale cheaply by default.** Generate drafts at 480p and upscale only the pieces that made the final cut. Use a local upscaler if the computer has a good GPU; paid upscaling only for a clip that needs it, with its own "yes".
6. **One video, not a season.** If an idea has several heroes or situations, they all go into one video. Don't stretch one plot into a series.

## Idea and story
7. **Stories come from real life, not from the head.** Take a real situation the user lived or saw, and give every idea two versions: the clear one and the unexpected one.
8. **Show concepts as options:** at least three, each with a different story engine, plus your pick with one reason. If a business is advertised, it must drive the plot: cut the business scenes and the story should fall apart.
9. **The ending is a turn.** Test it on paper before any credits. A video that simply stops (the hero leaves, fade to black) gives the viewer no reason to watch to the end. Worn memes need a new angle, or they read as "one more of those".
10. **Reveal nothing early, turn at the end.** The first second still hooks. Default: a short title card of 0.5-1 s in one house style at the start, and no other decorative titles.
11. **Show how the hero got here:** one to three shots of the fall before the main action.

## Picture and acting
12. **Emotion builds up:** closer framing toward the peak, the peak near the end, a quieter start. Emotions differ from shot to shot and read clearly. Name the emotion physically (mouth, eyes, neck) and let it play; don't freeze the face.
13. **Not "cute".** Faces match the approved character card and don't get younger or prettier from shot to shot. Skin is alive, no retouching (except a client's double, and only at their request).
14. **Default: absurd, not VFX.** Nothing comes alive, flies, glows or transforms. Test: could this be shot with real people and props?
15. **Default tone: comedy, not horror.** Big readable reactions. No red eyes, smeared mascara or dark dirty light unless the genre asks for it.
16. **The look follows the genre:** film stock for noir and heists, phone look for everyday comedy. Write it into the video's style block; one look per video.
17. **The camera is clean by default.** Slow motion up to 3 s, wide 14-24 mm lenses, snap zooms and one dolly zoom per video go only on gags and peaks, and are marked in the storyboard. Dialogue shots get a living camera: light handheld, slow push-ins, orbits. A locked camera only where stillness is the joke.
18. **Real people:** a photo pack first, then an approved portrait, then scenes. At most 2-3 named people per scene. Check likeness by putting the full-size crop next to the photo.
19. **Props and costumes as in real life.** No invented gadgets (police uniforms have no lights on them; officers carry flashlights).
20. **Check every frame at full size, and start frames in pairs,** before the user sees them: hands, doubles, direction of travel, same street or seat, real sizes.
21. **Overlays are designed graphics.** Titles, HUDs and badges drawn with code look cheap. Generate the graphic, show one sample frame first, then animate.

## Pace and edit
22. **No empty seconds.** Nobody walks in silence, nobody enters, nobody stares for long. Every shot starts with a line or a hit.
23. **Dense plot:** 15-20 events per video, a new event every 2-3 s, shots of 1.5-2.5 s, plus 3-4 long shots. An event means the situation changed; another crop of the same moment is not an event. Two beats in a row with the same feeling merge into one. Inside one place: wide → detail → reaction, each showing a new moment.

## Sound
24. **One voice per character** for the whole video.
25. **Move the voice only as whole lines, at speed 1.0.** Don't cut a phrase into pieces or stretch it for lip sync: put the first word on the frame where the mouth opens. Keep word tails; phrases never cut off.
26. **Music runs under the whole video and ducks under the voice.** Don't switch it off on every line. One hard stop at most, on the turn.
27. **Captions: one phrase on one line, one caption on screen.** Don't save a subtitle file next to the mp4 with the same name, or players show the captions twice.

## Working with the agent
28. **Study the material in full;** don't drop parts as "extra". Keep principles unchanged until the user changes them.
29. **The user's latest decision wins.** Don't remind them they said something else before.
30. **Every "don't do that" goes here with the date,** and into the project's `experience.md`.
