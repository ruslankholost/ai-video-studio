# 10 Publish and growth

A finished video earns nothing until people see it and remember it. This stage separates two things: the packaging of the video itself (hook, captions, struggle and turn) and the packaging of the author (profile, portfolio, series, platforms). Money from an audience comes in this order: viewers first, money second. The thresholds and claims below are claims made by creators and course material. They are not checked for your account or country, so check them on the platform pages before relying on them.

## Input and output

Input, from the project folder:
- The final video or series from stage 08, approved by the user.
- `script.md`, `storyboard.md`: needed to number episodes and write the hook at the end of each.
- `brief.md`, `idea.md`: who the series is for and the goal (followers, leads, sales).
- A list of reference accounts in the same niche, if the niche is new.

Output:
- `publish.md`: which platforms, in what order, with what title, episode number, cover, caption and time; the list of copies (if variants are needed) and where each lives; what to check in reach and when. (The fields are collected from the principles below; the structure is ours.)
- An updated portfolio and profile (bio, pinned posts, highlights).

## Principles

### Series or single videos

1. **For view-based payouts, make a series, not single videos.** People subscribe to a series, not to one video. A series needs recurring characters, a topic in which the viewer recognizes themselves within 3 seconds, and a hook at the end of every episode. (A course claim, not verified.)
2. **There are three income lines from cartoons and series:** payouts per view, commissions (companies order work), and integrations (a brand pays for a place in the story). Payouts and integrations need an audience, commissions need a portfolio, and the best portfolio is your own series with real views.
3. **A brand series and a micro-drama are separate ways to sell episodes.** Brands buy a "serial story" for reach and traffic. Foreign apps and platforms buy AI micro-dramas: episodes of 2-2.5 minutes, the first shown in ads, a cut-off on the climax, then a paid subscription. A pilot of 3 episodes of 60 seconds in one genre, posted to short-video feeds to see completion rate, is a cheap test.
4. **In a micro-drama, plot and intrigue beat visual perfection.** Viewers forgive a synthetic picture if the story hooks them.
5. **To a client, sell a series, not one video.** The client's goal (followers) is not reached with one video, so the client pays per month. A one-off video "for fun" costs about five times more, because reference cards must be built again for it. (Whether your own profile is a series or separate ideas is a strategy choice: see "Defaults" below.)

### Frequency and length

6. **To gather an audience for payouts:** 1-2 episodes a day, each at least 61 seconds, the episode number in the title, everything in a playlist, the same cartoon on all platforms including Instagram (where a client will see the author). The reason for exactly 61 seconds is not explained in the sources.
7. **For the first 1-3 months the only goal is followers.** Payouts come after the platform threshold.
8. **For an expert account (clients, not payouts) the rhythm differs:** Reels of 15-30 s 2-3 a week, carousels of 6-8 slides 1-2 a week, stories every day, a post once a week, 10-14 units a week in total. Regularity beats volume: 3 posts a week beats 10 in a month.
9. **For real client niches, keep the video strictly under a minute (about 55 seconds):** long videos get less reach. A series for a client is 4-5 videos a month.
10. **For a content batch on a subscription, the idea matters more than polish.** You need an idea that hooks in a second, not "wow quality". Find the format the audience answers, then multiply it. Publish in a batch to YouTube, TikTok and Instagram.

### The first second, retention within a series and between episodes

11. **The viewer has half a second, at most one second, to decide to watch or scroll.** Give the whole point in that second: what the video is about and why to stay.
12. **Do not tell everything in the first two seconds.** In a good story the hero struggles and there is a turn at the end. If everything is said at the start, there is nothing to feel.
13. **A long first frame kills the video.** A title card held for 4 seconds looks like a frozen phone. Start with action.
14. **The video must read without sound.** Many people watch muted. Large, high-contrast text in the platform's safe zone.
15. **Shorten the entrance for social feeds.** Wide establishing shots are too long for vertical video; cut them.
16. **Make the contrast between heroes strong,** so the viewer sees in seconds who is who. In 60-90 seconds subtle differences do not work.
17. **Keep the emotion close.** The viewer feels something concrete and understandable (a loss), not a nuclear war or the far future.
18. **In ads, show, do not invite.** Phrases like "we are waiting for you, come" work against you, because they say there are no customers. People go where people already are (social proof). The exception is a brand-lift video, where no story is needed.
19. **A hook for the next episode.** The end of every episode carries a hook. In a micro-drama the cut-off on the climax leads to the paid part. In a story-cartoon format the call can be a code word spoken by the hero, a different word for each episode.
20. **Number the episodes and use a playlist.** The episode number in the title, everything in one playlist. Numbered rubrics ("Dream No. 5") work the same way.

### Platforms and language

21. **Language decides whose views are paid.** English for TikTok in the US and UK. For other regions and for your own country (for example Canada), check the platform's pages for whether payouts apply.
22. **A narrow audience is easier to grow in.** Content for one nation or region has almost no competition. But a local story in English may fail: a local holiday means nothing to a foreign viewer.
23. **A hook for the audience of one city:** a video for a realtor opens with a panorama of the city and its name on screen.
24. **Think wider than one platform.** Content is needed wherever a business talks to clients.
25. **One content, several platforms, one title.** YouTube, TikTok and Instagram in a batch.

### Monetization thresholds (claims of the source, not ours)

26. **Numbers below come from lectures and disagree with each other.** Treat them as orientation only.

| Platform | Condition | What it pays |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok creator program | about 10 000 followers and 100 000 views in 30 days | per-view payout; the sources give different rates, so do not promise any to a client |
| TikTok LIVE | from about 500 followers | viewers send gifts that convert to money |
| TikTok sponsors | about 50 000 followers | paid integrations |
| YouTube Partner Program | 1 000 subscribers and 4 000 watch hours, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days | ad revenue per 1 000 views; sponsors |

YouTube requires an "AI-generated" label on such content. Main income is usually sponsors and your own products, not ads. Check each platform's current pages before planning around any number.

### When to change the niche

27. **There is no rule in the sources for "after how many videos change the niche", and we do not invent one.** Three nearby judgments exist:
    - Low reach: "it is not the algorithm or the platform, something is wrong with the content". Analyze which content earns reach and change the approach.
    - "First find a format the audience answers, then multiply it. Repeating 30 times what nobody cares about gives no result."
    - Pick a format that already pulls millions at other authors, and adapt it; do not squeeze a concept out of your head.
    A change of field can also be the author's own wish. The opposite advice: do not invent a new road, strengthen the one you already walk.

### Packaging the account

28. **The main mistake: the account looks like a catalog of works.** The client does not understand who you are, how you differ and why to come to you. Strong work without a personality and packaging does not bring clients.
29. **A catalog is for a portfolio** (for example a channel in a messenger). For an Instagram blog this format does not work.
30. **A blog matters because** when you answer a job post the client dictates terms, and when a client comes to you from social media you dictate them.
31. **Even a production company or agency should show themselves and the team.** The client needs to know whom exactly they are contacting.
32. **Serve each unit of content "in your own sauce".** Simple trends and basic photo shoots are made by many. Without ideas, ask the agent for creative ideas.
33. **Showing the beginner's path is fine.** But for clients to come, the service quality should be at least 3 out of 5.
34. **A bio with a result for the client and numbers:** "I create premium content for brands that increases reach and sales". It is stronger with numbers, because business people like what can be counted.
35. **Highlight covers:** a live photo and a clear label. Aesthetic icons and plain text do not work. Remove extra sections and update "about me".
36. **Five profile elements that work in 3 seconds:** positioning "I help [whom] get [result] through AI"; a 3-line bio (who, how useful, call to action); a photo with a face; highlights (About me, Cases, Services, Reviews); the first 9 posts (3 about you, 3 cases, 3 expert).
37. **Eight topics that bring clients:** how I did this with AI; before/after; tool of the week; a review of someone else's account; my result for a period; mistakes in the niche; what AI content costs; a client case (the most selling unit).
38. **80 percent value, 20 percent selling.** Clients can come at 200-500 followers; a case is the best content.
39. **The funnel:** content, trust (cases, expertise), engagement (stories, replies), request (call to action, direct message), purchase (contract, payment). Seven tactics: a call in every post, a pinned offer post, a case every week, stories with polls, free expert content, 15 minutes of commenting a day, partnerships.

### Portfolio

40. **A portfolio is not a gallery of 20 pictures but a story: task, method, result.** The client decides in 30 seconds.
41. **Seven required elements:** a cover (name, one-line specialization, style, call to action); "about me" as positioning (5-7 sentences); cases (ideal 5-8, "5 strong beat 20 weak"); services with prices or packages (never "price on request"); reviews and social proof; the process; contacts and an "Discuss a project" button.
42. **Add anything you are more or less happy with and do not wait for perfect.** Experience with a client matters more than perfect work. Describe the path, not only the result.
43. **Present each work as a case, not an example:** task, what was done, how long it took, result. A portfolio in a shared drive does not work; make a tidy page.
44. **One direction and a clear hit on the audience, no "everything at once".** A portfolio of mixed works has no value.
45. **A large visible project brings orders.** One author reported hundreds of messages after a series release, several paid consultations and three projects, without direct money from views.
46. **Collaboration posts on Instagram across several accounts merge the statistics.** One series published on five accounts reached 500 000 people, though most of them were small.
47. **Before release, agree on community support:** reposts and boosts by fellow creators in the first hours.
48. **Breakdowns of works are a content form.** A free public mini-audit of someone else's account makes the viewer think "I want that".
49. **If you are shy to show yourself, work on it.**

#### Portfolio in detail

- Why: most hiring people prefer candidates with a portfolio. Without one you are a voice in a chat; with one you are a professional judged in 30 seconds. A portfolio is a silent seller that works 24/7.
- The client does not care how many works you have, which tools you know, or how pretty the page is. They care whether you solved a similar task, what result the previous client got, and whether you can be trusted.
- Cover (the first 5 seconds): name or pseudonym, one-line specialization, visual style, a call to look or contact. Not: "Hi, I am Masha, I am 25", a list of 15 tools, stock photos, "I am creative and responsible".
- "About me" is positioning, not a biography. Five lines: what you do; experience in numbers; whom you work for; method; client result. "Responsible and punctual" is the minimum, not a quality.
- A case has four blocks: TASK, PROCESS, RESULT (numbers, screenshots, visuals), REVIEW (client's words). A story of solving a task, not screenshots.
- Services and prices: use packages (S/M/L) or "from" with examples. "Price on request" loses most clients. Transparency builds trust, and trust sells.
- Reviews: your words about yourself are marketing, the client's words about you are proof. Works: chat screenshots, 30-second video reviews, text with the client's name and niche, numbers. Does not work: "great, thanks", reviews without a name, invented reviews. No reviews yet: do 3 projects free or at a symbolic price; a review is currency at the start.
- Process: brief (a short questionnaire), strategy (approve a plan), work, delivery (corrections and final). The more open the process, the fewer questions and the faster the payment.
- Contacts: one main messenger, email for corporate clients, a visible "Discuss a project" button on every page. Remove vague "contact me", unused forms and 8 social networks (2-3 are enough).
- A messenger channel is a good free portfolio start: pinned positioning, case posts regularly, behind the scenes, useful content, reviews, and "write to me" in every fifth post. A simple notes page can grow into a site builder page later.
- Where to find clients: freelance marketplaces, messenger chats where you show expertise in discussions, professional networks (B2B and international clients), meetups (one talk can bring several requests), your own blog (warm clients). Repeat clients are most of freelance income, and referrals are the best source of new ones.
- Method "10 letters": find 10 experts or brands, study their blog, write "I see [problem]. Here is how I can help: [solution]. Here is my case: [link]". Not "Hello, I am an AI creator, I have services".
- Five mistakes: 50 works without context (you need 5-8 stories); "I know 15 tools" instead of a task, a result in numbers and your taste; price on request; no niche ("a niche does not limit, it focuses, and you can widen later"); "made it and forgot it". Update rules: add a case the same day; once a month remove weak ones and add strong ones; once a quarter update prices, services and description; add every review at once.
- Start today, not "when it is perfect" or "when I have 20 cases". Minimum for one day: a channel with pinned positioning; 3 cases (even practice ones); "about me" by the formula; prices and a call to action; the link sent to 10 potential clients. No cases: do 3 projects free for a friend or an acquaintance.
- A week plan: day 1 platform and "about me"; days 2-3 three to five cases; day 4 services, prices, process, reviews; day 5 contacts and call to action, check on a phone, show a friend; day 6 two freelance profiles with a link to the portfolio; day 7 ten letters.
- Closing thought: tools are the same for everyone. Uniqueness is in your style, taste and approach.

### Re-uploads and uniquifying

50. **A mistake found in a published video:** re-uploading the same file may cut reach heavily (claimed up to 90 percent). Make a changed version instead (see 53).
51. **Be honest about the limit.** There is no guarantee that Instagram accepts a copy as new. It looks for repeats with a model trained on such edits and compares sound separately.
52. **Own content only.** Other people's videos are not uniquified. Meta has content protection, and the author gets a notice about a copy.
53. **What really helps:** your own sound (new music or a new voice-over), 30 percent or more of new material (a different hook in the first 2-3 seconds, inserts, a different ending), no more than 10 similar videos per account in 30 days, copies on different accounts and on different days, not in a batch.
54. **Single tricks work poorly:** only crop, only mirror, only noise, only metadata cleaning, only a changed caption and hashtags. A combination of picture, sound and file helps. Never mirror (it flips text and does not fool the model).
55. **Never count a batch as done blind:** every copy has a different checksum and has sound, none is larger than the source, the contact sheet was viewed, and the report says "sound not checked by ear". (Building copies is a local ffmpeg job that the agent can script; there is no ready script in this repo.)
56. **Instagram rules on unoriginal content (April 2024):** an account with 10 or more such videos in 30 days drops out of recommendations. Do not present a "70 percent match" threshold from practitioners as an official number.

### What reviews of members' accounts and works fixed

57. **Five account mistakes (a review of 7 profiles):** a faceless catalog profile; "look what I can do" (everything mixed: cartoon, holiday greeting, trends); highlight covers explain nothing; low reach blamed on the algorithm; work level below the declared one. Fixes: add stories, intro posts, describe services, show yourself and the team; serve in your own way; live covers; analyze what earns reach; improve skills.
58. **What worked well:** personality in the profile, a talking head, live streams, reviews, a service list; the beginner's path as content; a bio with a result; one style of the feed and designed highlights.
59. **An expert account should have:** tricks and tips; cases and portfolio; the work process; about you (who you are and why to trust you); your path. Through a blog you can sell consultations, one-off projects, content management turnkey, and teaching.
60. **Review of works (set 1, 8 videos):** the usual mistake is "the picture is there, the meaning is not". Models render a picture and you invent the meaning. One generation of 15 seconds from one prompt gives doubles; better to make several variants and cut together the best frames into 10, 15 or 20 seconds. Look into the camera only when the hero speaks to the viewer, or once in the final.
61. **Review of works (set 2, 10 videos):** a weak start, no struggle and no turn, everything told in the first two seconds, no captions, same-type frames, generation glitches. Fixes were given per video.
62. **A narrow topic and one direction bring clients faster than "everything at once".**

## Defaults (resolved for this studio; the user may change them)

- Series or separate ideas: the studio default is a recognizable author profile with one visual style and, if the user wants, a recurring hero. Each video is a finished story. Two kinds of posts: the video itself and a "how it was made" breakdown (references, previz and result). The user decides whether to build one series instead. (default; the user may change it)
- Length and frequency: 30-60 seconds, about three videos a week with breakdowns between. 61 seconds and 1-2 a day only if the user chases payouts. (default; the user may change it)
- Title card: no decorative titles or fonts unless the user asks. One short rubric line of 0.5-1 seconds in one consistent style is allowed. Speech subtitles are plain, readable and on when anyone speaks. The first second must read without sound from the action itself.
- Copies: at most 10 similar videos per account in 30 days, a different sound and a new hook in each copy. Do not present the numbers 90 percent or 70 percent as official.
- Payouts: write the threshold and range as claims of the source. Never promise them to a client.
- Do not sign posts with the tools used, unless there is a partnership or the post is a breakdown.

## Workflow

1. Decide the goal of publishing: view payouts (series, audience), client requests (expert profile and cases), or both. The goal sets frequency and length (principles 6-10).
2. Check the video against the list below: first second, captions, struggle and turn, cuts.
3. Name the series: number in the title, playlist, one title on all platforms (principles 6, 20). **User's ok.**
4. Decide language and platforms (principles 21-25, table). Mark for the user which platforms really pay them.
5. Prepare copies if a re-upload or versions for other accounts are needed (principles 50-56).
6. Prepare collaboration and support: a list of accounts for Instagram collaboration and people who support in the first hours (principles 46-47).
7. Publish, only when the user says so. Write platforms, time and links into `publish.md`.
8. Add the work to the portfolio the same day as a case (task, what was done, how long, result).
9. Check the profile against the list below.
10. After a few days, review which video earned reach and make more like it.

## Formulas

Bio:

```
I help [whom] get [result] through AI
```

Bio with numbers: "I create premium content for brands that increases reach and sales" plus real figures.

Offer:

```
I make [product] for [niche] so that [result].
```

Good: "AI photo shoots for beauticians so they can run a blog without filming". Bad: "I make neural networks, photos and video for everyone".

Case: Task (what the client needed), What was done (your steps), How long (terms), Result (views, requests, sales).

Hook formulas for an expert blog (any topic becomes "a story with conflict and detail", not "advice"):

```
1. Experiment: "I tried [trend/tool] for [period] and this happened"
2. Life hack: "This [X]-second trick gives [result] almost instantly"
3. Insight: "Nobody says that [A] + [B] = [unexpected result]"
4. Pain to solution: "For [X] months I suffered from [pain]; three things changed everything"
5. Myth: "[Belief] is a total lie. Here is what works"
6. From zero: "If I started again, here is what I would do differently"
7. Income: "I earned [sum] with [unusual method]. Here is how"
8. Secret: "Everyone talks about [trend], nobody shares this"
9. Counter-position: "Most people do X. I do Y. The difference is [result]"
```

Use formula 7 only with a real number. Never invent figures.

Day-one portfolio, week plan and 30-day blog plan: see "Portfolio in detail" above. 30-day blog plan: days 1-3 foundation (positioning, bio, highlights, pinned post); days 4-7 nine showcase posts; days 8-14 rhythm (3 posts a week, 1 reel, daily stories, 15 minutes of commenting); days 15-21 requests (calls in posts, stories with questions); days 22-30 the first client (1-2 clients from 10 dialogs, a review, a case).

## Check before "ok"

Video and series:
- [ ] The first second says what the video is about
- [ ] There is a struggle and a turn, and the turn is not shown at the start
- [ ] People's emotions are visible, not only landscapes
- [ ] Shot sizes alternate; no three identical frames in a row
- [ ] References were in high resolution; glitches were regenerated
- [ ] Cuts were reviewed; no half-cut frames
- [ ] Text does not lie on a face and is readable; text does not replace the frame
- [ ] The video reads without sound
- [ ] The meaning is stated in one sentence; the product follows from the story; the storyboard came before generation; prompts say where the character looks; several variants were cut together; color does not jump
- [ ] The end of the series has a hook for the next episode
- [ ] The episode number is in the title and a playlist exists

Profile:
- [ ] The bio says who I am and what result a client gets
- [ ] The bio has numbers: reach, sales, client results
- [ ] My face and my story are in the profile, not only works
- [ ] It is clear which services I provide and how to contact me
- [ ] Highlight covers have live photos and clear names
- [ ] "About me" is updated
- [ ] The content has its own delivery, not only trends
- [ ] The feed has cases, process, useful content and my path
- [ ] I analyzed which content earns reach and I make more of it
- [ ] The catalog of works is moved to a separate portfolio

Before publishing copies: every checksum is different, every copy has sound, none is larger than the source, the contact sheet was viewed, and "sound not checked by ear" is in the report.

Checklist for a first big project: watched stages 01-08; script, character vibe and references ready before the first generation; time and credits for searching and redoing planned in the budget; contrast between heroes readable in seconds; entrance shortened for feeds; prepayment and number of corrections written in the contract; generation costs agreed to be paid by the client; the video posted as a collaboration with all participants; the community asked for support in the first hours.

The stage closes only with the user's "ok".

## Common mistakes

- Account as a catalog, "look what I can do", highlight covers that explain nothing, low reach blamed on the algorithm, work below the declared level.
- The character looks into the camera all the time; no plot and no storyboard; product unrelated to the story; a video from one generation; a weak hook.
- Weak start, no struggle and turn, no captions, a long first frame, landscapes without emotion, same-type frames, symmetric faces, half-cut joins, "we are waiting for you", an English version of a local story.
- Re-uploading the same file (reach may drop sharply).
- Portfolio: 50 works instead of 5-8; "I know 15 tools" instead of a result; "price on request" (claimed to lose most clients); no niche; "made it and forgot it".
- Blog: writing for everyone; only selling; waiting for an audience; no results; chaos.
- Seven income brakes: not packaging the portfolio and waiting for "when I am ready", among others.
- An AI-blogger account can be blocked during a platform cleanup. Keep copies of metrics and materials, and collect contacts outside the platform.
- Information overload; wanting perfect ("perfect is the enemy of result").
