# Laws of the studio

The 48 load-bearing laws of the method, collected from the principles of the 12 stage documents in `docs/stages/`. In every law, "Stages" lists the principle numbers (stage.principle, for example 06.12) where the details live. Where the method and the studio's taste choices differ, the studio default is written here. The user may change a taste default.

---

## I. Idea and preproduction

**1. Preproduction decides half the result; do not rush to generations.**
Why: production and postproduction do not fix mistakes in the idea or script, they enlarge them. Rough weights: preproduction 50 percent, production 25, postproduction 25. A reroll cures a prompt mistake, not a script mistake.
Stages: 00.3, 01.6, 09.7, 09.59, 01.4, 00.5

**2. The idea is written in one sentence before the first generation: who, what they want, what stops them.**
Why: without a story you get a set of beautiful frames with no meaning, and meaning is what sets a creator apart.
Stages: 00.1, 00.2, 00.8, 00.4, 01.1, 01.8

**3. One idea per video, one strange thing, a retelling in one sentence.**
Why: several ideas and several tricks cancel each other, and a scene built on one thought is easier to generate. The strange thing is an absurd situation played straight with no special effects: nothing comes alive, flies or transforms. (default; the user may change it)
Stages: 00.11, 00.19, 08.80, 01.14, 00.18

**4. Meaning first, then the frame. The human builds the story, emotion and logic.**
Why: the model executes what is written and does not invent meaning, and plot and intrigue matter more than the beauty of a picture.
Stages: 01.16, 02.1, 00.46, 00.54, 00.7, 10.60, 08.21

**5. On production we invent nothing, only execute. If we compose on the fly, we go back to preproduction.**
Why: the model carries out what is written and guesses everything else, usually badly.
Stages: 01.2, 01.3, 01.14, 01.71, 02.20, 09.59

**6. Frame format, duration and number of shots are fixed before the first generation.**
Why: a format change means every generation again.
Stages: 00.9, 01.9, 09.8

**7. The hook is built at the idea stage: from the first second it is clear what the video is about, and it reads without sound.**
Why: the viewer decides to watch or scroll in half a second, and a long first frame looks like a frozen phone.
Stages: 00.31, 00.32, 00.33, 00.34, 01.52, 08.10, 08.11, 10.11, 10.13

**8. Do not tell everything in the first seconds: the hero struggles and the end turns.**
Why: if everything is said at the start, there is nothing to feel. The first second still hooks with a question or a hit, but the outcome stays hidden and the turn comes at the end (`docs/lessons-learned.md`, 9-10).
Stages: 00.36, 08.12, 10.12, 10.61, 00.37

**9. The idea is tested for generatability and on real people before production.**
Why: a weak idea should die for free, not after the money is spent. The agent counts generations for itself during ideation and quotes credits to the user only once the story is approved.
Stages: 00.10, 00.21, 00.23, 00.24, 01.26, 01.72

**10. A series matters more than a single video.**
Why: people subscribe to a series with recurring heroes, not to one video. For a client a series is paid per month, and reference cards are built once. The argument rests on subscription and recurring heroes, not on payouts per view. Each episode is still a complete story: a series repeats heroes and format, it does not stretch one plot across episodes (`docs/lessons-learned.md`, 6).
Stages: 00.48, 00.49, 00.51, 10.1, 10.5, 10.19, 10.20

**11. Facts and promises are real: numbers, reviews and cases come from sources.**
Why: one wrong number ruins a video, and invented promises turn the video into the thing it mocks.
Stages: 00.30, 09.12, 08.87, 08.89, 08.91

## II. References and frames

**12. Every reference has a role: what we take and what we do NOT take.**
Why: without roles the model blends references into mush, and an object card drags its own shooting environment (background, studio) into the frame.
Stages: 03.31, 03.32, 03.33, 03.34, 03.67, 04.21, 04.22, 06.4, 06.5

**13. A reference is structure, not someone else's brand. Take tempo and moves; do not take logos, texts, characters or music.**
Why: others' material is not carried over to a client, and the model can copy a reference directly, down to another person's face.
Stages: 00.26, 00.27, 01.36, 01.37, 06.62, 08.84, 07.68

**14. Details come from a moodboard of real samples, not from the head.**
Why: this makes a frame differ from the average result, and color and style do not jump between frames.
Stages: 01.27, 01.28, 01.29, 01.31, 01.32, 01.40, 04.6, 02.21

**15. A face is held only by a reference (image or video), not by a text description.**
Why: "brown eyes, dark hair" fits a million people, one angle is not enough, and in video the face drifts.
Stages: 03.1, 03.2, 03.3, 03.35, 03.36, 04.25, 04.26, 04.32, 06.9

**16. Everything that repeats in the video gets a reference card. Face and body are made separately.**
Why: without a card, every frame has a different car, a different jacket, a different face. A face assembled from several angles in one image gets rebuilt every time.
Stages: 03.17, 03.18, 03.19, 03.21, 03.25, 03.26, 04.23, 04.24

**17. The establishing frame is the anchor of the scene. Budget for many rerolls: up to 95 percent of generations can be rejected.**
Why: reference cards hold faces and things but not the world. One frame with character, location, light and style holds the whole scene.
Stages: 03.41, 03.42, 03.43, 03.44, 04.30, 04.31, 01.79

**18. Corrections: change one variable at a time, and each correction has two parts, "what to change" and "what to keep".**
Why: without a "keep" list a correction breaks the rest, and a series of frames loses unity.
Stages: 03.11, 03.51, 03.52, 04.9, 04.14, 04.16, 04.18, 04.27

**19. A picture storyboard is for the human to see the story. Its frames are not fed to the video model.**
Why: what goes into the video model are reference cards of characters, locations and objects that run through the video; otherwise the model jumps and duplicates.
Stages: 01.66, 03.38, 03.39, 06.8, 09.59

**20. A storyboard and the user's "ok" come before any paid generation.**
Why: a picture is cheap to fix, a finished animation is expensive in time and credits. An "ok" for one run does not carry to the next. The price message names the model, the number of jobs and the credits from the estimate.
Stages: 01.67, 04.53, 06.48, 07.81, 08.61, 08.71, 08.82, 09.53, 09.57

## III. Camera and video

**21. The camera is the hardest part. A long take or a hard move is caught in previz; a simple move is held by the start frame and the prompt.**
Why: a prompt hardly holds a long or complex camera. In previz (Blender) the video is visible before generation and is fixed in words for free, and the video model receives finished motion, paths and speed. Short shots with a static camera, a slow push-in or a pan do not need it (stage 05, "When this stage is needed").
Stages: 06.13, 05.1, 05.2, 05.8, 05.15, 05.16, 02.40, 01.72

**22. The camera is always set and explicit: the angle first, the type of movement named.**
Why: what you do not set, you get at random. A named angle and movement cut the number of attempts.
Stages: 06.10, 06.11, 06.12, 02.34, 02.35, 02.41, 01.43, 01.51, 02.9, 05.34

**23. One shot: one action, one camera move.**
Why: two actions or two moves give flicker, and the model gets confused.
Stages: 02.36, 02.14, 06.14, 06.15, 06.28, 05.26b

**24. Shot size alternates: wide, medium, close, detail. A whole video in one size is the main mistake.**
Why: the eye gets used to it and attention leaves. Each size answers its own viewer question: where are we, who, what do they feel, what to look at.
Stages: 01.44, 01.45, 01.46, 02.2, 02.4, 02.22, 08.13, 08.14, 08.23

**25. Emotion beats everything else: describe it physically (brows, eyes, mouth, neck) and hold it in the edit.**
Why: the model draws what is visible, not the word "scared". In a cut, emotion weighs 51 percent, story 23, rhythm 10. Build it up: a quieter start, closer framing toward the peak, the peak near the end (`docs/lessons-learned.md`, 12).
Stages: 02.46, 02.47, 02.48, 02.49, 01.56, 06.17, 08.19, 08.30, 08.31, 07.62

**26. Light decides the frame more than anything else and is named in every segment: source, direction, temperature.**
Why: light turns an "average photo" into a frame, and "cinematic lighting" without a source is ignored by the model.
Stages: 02.13, 02.28, 02.29, 02.30, 02.32, 04.3, 04.4, 01.64, 06.39

**27. Realism comes from concrete facts and imperfections: handheld, skin texture, documentary details. Empty words are not written.**
Why: "beautiful" and "perfect" lead to the uncanny valley. Camera shake written in the prompt looks better than shake added in the edit. References that are too clean give plastic in video.
Stages: 01.57, 02.38, 02.52, 02.53, 02.57, 02.60, 03.40, 04.8, 04.35, 04.49, 04.57

**28. Describe what must be there; do not forbid. A negative is only a concrete one for the scene.**
Why: prohibitions ("no blur") are read poorly, and a generic junk negative ("extra fingers") does nothing. Models with their own negative block follow their own template.
Stages: 06.35, 06.36, 06.37, 09.45, 07.21, 08.55

**29. Cheap first (a draft, a short test), then finishing.**
Why: a draft ordered in draft mode is finished at full resolution as the same take, not as a new one.
Stages: 06.47, 05.32, 09.54, 08.46, 09.55

**30. Regenerate only the bad shot, and collect corrections into one message.**
Why: one regeneration with five corrections is cheaper than five single ones, and every generation costs credits.
Stages: 06.49, 06.50, 08.62, 08.83, 09.15, 09.82, 01.77

## IV. Sound and post

**31. One voice per hero: the voice is chosen and fixed before the series, and voicing is planned in the storyboard.**
Why: the viewer recognizes a hero by ear. A voice from a video-model prompt cannot be fixed, so for a series the voice is replaced after generation or a ready one is supplied as input.
Stages: 07.2, 07.3, 07.4, 07.7, 07.11, 07.33, 01.59

**32. Sound is written inside each shot, the line verbatim in quotes with its language. Music is not ordered in generation, it is laid in the edit.**
Why: sound as one line at the end of a Seedance prompt is ignored. Music in generation hides the voice and noises, and a separate track can be replaced in minutes.
Stages: 06.30, 06.31, 06.32, 07.13, 07.18, 07.66, 07.63, 07.67, 08.32

**33. Postproduction order: edit, sound, color. We go past the rough cut only when the story reads without sound and color.**
Why: coloring and voicing frames that will be cut is pointless, and a mistake missed earlier is the most expensive to fix at the edit.
Stages: 08.1, 08.2, 08.3, 08.7, 08.4, 08.5

**34. Upscale only accepted frames, and always: before effects, grain after.**
Why: improving rejects is pointless, and upscalers eat film grain laid earlier. Upscale every accepted frame, only after acceptance.
Stages: 04.36, 04.37, 04.38, 06.53, 08.44, 08.45, 08.46, 05.42

**35. The agent does not hear sound and does not see the screen: check by numbers and a frame grid, and say honestly "not checked by ear".**
Why: the agent sees video at about 5 frames per second and can miss text on a face, while loudness and a spectrogram are checked with tools.
Stages: 07.80, 08.38, 08.40, 08.56, 08.64, 09.11, 09.52, 06.52, 03.68

**36. A video must read without sound.**
Why: many people watch muted. By default the frame and the action carry the meaning (law 7); plain speech subtitles are on when someone speaks, and decorative titles and fonts are not added unless the user asks. One short rubric line is allowed. (default; the user may change it)
Stages: 07.79, 08.49, 08.50, 08.51, 08.53, 10.14, 00.33

## V. Project and agent

**37. The user is the director, the agent is the executor: the human sets the task, looks, corrects and says "ok". Taste stays with the human.**
Why: the agent takes the manual work but does not replace the eye and taste, and it does not spend money on its own.
Stages: 09.1, 01.5, 00.60, 01.82, 09.57, 04.53

**38. One video = one folder = one agent session. One folder order for every project.**
Why: after a month there are dozens of folders, and in a long chat the agent rereads the whole conversation on every step, which gets expensive.
Stages: 08.60, 09.13, 09.14, 09.16, 09.17, 09.82, 08.63

**39. Nothing is overwritten. A new version gets a new name, rejects go to drafts, not the bin.**
Why: a good frame from an old take can always be brought back. All prompts live in one file with the current version in place.
Stages: 05.28, 06.51, 09.18, 09.19, 09.20, 09.37, 04.17

**40. Rules are written once in `AGENTS.md`, and every "do not do that" is appended there with a date. Experience goes to the project's experience file.**
Why: the agent then does not repeat mistakes, and style does not have to be explained again in every video.
Stages: 09.22, 09.26, 09.27, 09.29, 09.31, 08.59, 00.61, 09.32

**41. A skill for every model, because the agent's knowledge of models goes stale.**
Why: the agent's training data is old, so without a skill it gives something generic. A skill is built once and grows. Before a task the agent checks the current models and balance and never guesses ids. The formula is common across models, while limits and tags of a model sit in a thin layer in its skill.
Stages: 09.60, 09.61, 09.62, 09.63, 09.65, 04.10, 04.11, 04.54, 09.73

**42. API keys live in one `.env` and never enter chat or other files. A password is never given to the agent.**
Why: a key can spend the user's money.
Stages: 09.33, 09.23, 08.88, 09.71, 09.72

## VI. Money and growth

**43. The client pays for generations, and search and redoing are put into the estimate once the idea and script are agreed.**
Why: up to 95 percent of generations are rejected, and one real series project needed over two hundred video runs. This is about client projects; the user's own credits fall under law 20.
Stages: 11.43, 11.44, 11.41, 11.45, 11.42, 00.55, 00.56, 01.79, 01.80

**44. We sell a result and a series, not "skill in one model" and not single videos.**
Why: an entrepreneur can do simple things alone. A client pays for a month and for followers, and a package is cheaper than "per piece" only in appearance.
Stages: 11.1, 11.2, 11.8, 11.9, 11.34, 11.35, 10.5, 00.49

**45. One direction and one niche; start from what you already know.**
Why: you do not need to study 20 directions, and a "everything at once" portfolio carries no value.
Stages: 11.13, 11.55, 11.57, 10.44, 10.62, 08.85, 00.65

**46. You need hundreds of attempts, not ten, and no waiting for the perfect moment.**
Why: ten mailings without an answer are not yet a result, and experience with a client matters more than perfect work.
Stages: 11.60, 11.61, 11.62, 10.42, 00.66, 08.85

**47. The profile is built on a person and a result, not on a catalog of works.**
Why: the client does not understand who you are and why to come to you. The main account mistake is a catalog. One author, different videos.
Stages: 10.28, 10.29, 10.31, 10.36, 10.57, 00.62, 00.63

**48. Only your own content, and honest limits: other people's videos are not uniquified, and there is no guarantee that Instagram accepts a copy.**
Why: Meta has content protection, and Instagram's model looks for repeats. A combination helps (new sound, 30 percent new material); single tricks do not.
Stages: 08.90, 08.91, 08.92, 08.95, 10.50, 10.51, 10.52, 10.53, 10.55
